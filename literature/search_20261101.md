# Literature Search: 2026-11-01

Second literature pass, run after the 2026-10-27 baseline (33 entries in
`references.bib`). Purpose: find what the current eight work packages **do not
cover**, not to re-confirm what they already cite.

## Method and honest boundary

- Tool: `paper_search` over 20 sources (Crossref, ACM DL, IEEE, OpenAlex, DOAJ,
  Europe PMC, PubMed, PubMed Central, arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, ChemRxiv,
  Semantic Scholar, CORE, Zenodo, HAL, IACR ePrint, OpenReview, SSRN, Unpaywall)
  plus 3 Indonesian portals.
- CPU-only. No model API, no paid service, no PDF corpus download.
- **Metadata-only.** Titles, venues, DOIs, arXiv identifiers, and author lists
  come from the index APIs. **Full texts were not read in this pass.** Any
  quantitative claim below is what the *abstract* reports, not something this
  repository verified. Claims promoted into a paper still need a full-text audit.
- Where an author list was not returned by the API, the field is recorded as
  `null` rather than guessed. Anonymous OpenReview submissions are marked
  `anonymous`.

## Gap G1 — nothing in the portfolio measures a *series*

The central question says "over a long series of tasks", but CLEAR-300-v1 is 300
**independent** draws scored per task. No decay curve, no carryover state, no
meltdown onset. This is the largest gap and the one the literature has moved on
from most recently.

- **Beyond pass@1: A Reliability Science Framework for Long-Horizon LLM Agents**
  — arXiv `2603.29231`, DOI `10.48550/arxiv.2603.29231`. Proposes a reliability
  decay curve, a variance amplification factor, a graceful-degradation score, and
  a meltdown-onset point; abstract reports 10 models / 396 tasks / 23,392
  episodes, with graceful-degradation dropping from 0.90 to 0.44 in the software
  domain. *Closest single precedent to P9; adopt the metric vocabulary and
  compare against it rather than inventing a parallel one.*
- **Staying on Task: Testing the Foundations of Long-Horizon Agent Reliability**
  — arXiv `2609.38712`. Long-transduction diagnostic.
- **Long-Horizon Reliability in Human–LLM Interaction** — SSRN
  `10.2139/ssrn.6368071`. Qualitative failure taxonomy; introduces "narrative arc
  confabulation".
- **Invisible AI Failure** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.6491218`. 76,514 messages /
  226 sessions / 3,226 hours of sustained interaction.
- **LADDER: A Long-horizon Robot Manipulation Benchmark** — OpenReview
  (anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission). 1→10 subgoals, 145 tasks, 2,024 episodes per
  system; abstract reports low-level policies at 85.4% on single-subgoal tasks
  falling to near zero on extended ones. *Empirical horizon-decay evidence outside
  our domain; useful as an external anchor for "the drop is real, not our harness".*
- **Tapestry: Enabling Continuous Collaboration Across Episodes** — OpenReview
  (anonymous, ICLR 2027). Argues existing benchmarks **isolate episodes** and
  makes task streams continuous in evolving environments. *The design precedent
  for CLEAR-300-L.*
- **Web2LongTraj** — OpenReview (anonymous, ICLR 2027). Two operators,
  `AnchorStretch` (extends dependency distance by inserting episodes between a
  premise and its dependent later step) and `ArtifactStitch`. *A concrete,
  reusable mechanism for manufacturing cross-session dependency on CPU.*
- **MAPLEBench** — OpenReview (anonymous, ICLR 2027). Long-horizon leakage that
  "may emerge, propagate, or **resurface** across extended interactions".
- **CoEmbod** — EMNLP 2026, DOI `10.18653/2026.emnlp-main.2247`. Long-horizon
  multi-agent cooperation with explicit inter-agent dependencies.
- **AutoWorldModel-Bench** — arXiv `2608.11216` (Moodi; Zhu; de Mesentier Silva
  et al.). Closed-loop, fixed compute budget, 64 sessions. *Relevant to P4 + P8:
  a fixed-budget improvement loop is exactly the shape P8 lacks.*
- **What Reliability Metrics Miss: Evidence from NJ Transit** — SSRN
  `10.2139/ssrn.7118580`. On-time performance explains lateness but not
  mechanical breakdown; a second metric is needed. *Cheap, citable analogy for our
  argument that success-rate and degradation are different quantities.*

## Gap G2 — memory work tests immediate poison, not the memory lifecycle

P3/ProvMem covers flat vs layered poison and gets a clean 1.0 → 0.0 contrast. It
does not cover **when** the poison fires, what consolidation does to provenance,
or whether deletion works.

- **Hidden in Memory: Sleeper Memory Poisoning in LLM Agents** — arXiv
  `2605.15338`. Delayed trigger, dormant across sessions, re-emerges later.
- **Memory Provenance Laundering in LLM Agents** — arXiv `2607.29167`.
  Consolidation strips provenance so downstream trust decisions inherit poisoned
  state. *Direct attack on our layered-provenance claim: our 1.0 → 0.0 result
  measures injection-time provenance, not post-consolidation provenance.*
- **Deletion Is Not Forgetting: Behavioral Residuals of Deleted Memories** — SSRN
  `10.2139/ssrn.7428966`. Challenges the active-forgetting assumption. *New
  hypothesis H19.*
- **PACMI: Provenance-Aware Cascading Memory Invalidation** — SSRN
  `10.2139/ssrn.7480138`. Dependency graph + four-state validity lattice for
  stale-memory invalidation.
- **MemMorph** — arXiv `2605.26154`. Memory poisoning that hijacks tool choice.
- **PipePoison** — arXiv `2609.00523`. End-to-end indirect poisoning.
- **Retrieval Beats Cheap Structured Memory** — preprint
  `10.20944/preprints202608.1369.v1`. On LoCoMo/LongMemEval, BM25-style retrieval
  is reported at ~98% fewer tokens and ~4.8% of the cost. *Counter-evidence
  against elaborate memory layers — worth a section, and worth a cheap ablation
  of our own three-tier memory.*

## Gap G3 — the self-evolution direction is empirically contested

Our harness accumulates rules, skills, and memory across tasks. The literature
now says that accumulation may *cost* capability, which we have never measured.

- **Are Self-Evolving Agents Continual Learners?** — arXiv `2610.25858`. Tests
  ReasoningBank/GEPA/Meta-Harness on drifting streams (BIRD/BFCL/ScienceWorld)
  and finds they are **not** effective continual learners. *Strongest single
  challenge to the "the harness gets better over time" assumption.*
- **Do Self-Evolving Agents Forget? Capability Degradation and Preservation** —
  arXiv `2605.09315`. Capability erosion across workflow / skill / model / memory
  channels, plus a stabilization procedure.
- **From a Prompt to Repertoires** — arXiv `2610.11373`. Prompt optimization
  causes catastrophic forgetting.
- **Forget to Improve: On-Device LLM-Agent Continual Learning via
  Budget-Curated Memory** — arXiv `2606.25115`. `net-value-per-byte` curation
  with KEEP/evict under RAM and energy budgets. *Joins P3 with P8.*
- **Safety in Self-Evolving Agents: A Survey** — `10.13140/rg.2.2.23319.74403`
  (Chen; Feng; Ma et al.). Framing worth quoting: once experience becomes
  reusable state, "past events become future causes".
- **A Comprehensive Survey of Forgetting in Deep Learning Beyond Continual
  Learning** — TPAMI 2024, DOI `10.1109/tpami.2024.3498346`. Forgetting as a
  double-edged property.
- **PTCG-Bench** — Findings of EMNLP 2026, DOI `10.18653/2026.findings-emnlp.1689`.
  Abstract reports "sustained and stable self-evolution remains challenging".

## Gap G4 — verifier gap and evidence gate are studied separately

P1 (verifier gap) and P5 (negative-control evidence gate) are the two halves of
one claim. Recent work makes the connection explicit and gives it a theory.

- **Verifier Errors in RLVR: Reward Hacking, Limits of Feedback, and Selective
  Control** — OpenReview. Argues the observations available *during* RLVR are
  insufficient to identify accepted-but-wrong outputs, so external audit feedback
  is required. *This is the P1 × P5 bridge, and it is a stronger version of our
  own claim.*
- **Reward Hacking in Rubric-Based RL** — arXiv `2605.12474`. Separates verifier
  failure from rubric-design limitation, using a three-judge cross-family panel.
- **RL with Verifiable yet Noisy Rewards under Imperfect Verifiers** — arXiv
  `2510.00915`. Asymmetric false-positive/false-negative rates with
  backward/forward correction.
- **Gradient Regularization Mitigates Reward Hacking** — arXiv `2602.18037`.
- **Why the Gain of RLVR Does Not Decompose** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.7346356`.
  Pre-registered intervention study; interaction term dominates. *Methodological
  resonance with P7b factorial attribution.*
- **Near-Miss: Latent Policy Failure Detection in Agentic Workflows** — arXiv
  `2603.29665`; also DOI `10.18653/v1/2026.gem-main.30`. Defines the case where
  an agent **skips a required check but gets the right answer by luck**. *Our
  evidence gate scores the artifact, not the path; this is the missing axis.*

## Gap G5 — our metrics have never been tested for validity

We have 190 tests over 33 files proving the harness runs deterministically. That
is not the same as proving a metric moves when the failure moves. The literature
has turned this into a field.

- **WorkflowPerturb: Calibrated Stress Tests for Evaluating Multi-Agent Workflow
  Metrics** — EMNLP 2026 industry track, DOI `10.18653/2026.emnlp-industry.25`
  (Kanda; Agarwal; Fonseca et al.). 4,973 golden workflows × 44,757 graded
  perturbations, used to *calibrate the metrics themselves*. *Direct template for
  P12.*
- **Blind to the Pivotal Vote: Aggregate Independence Metrics Miss Where
  Verification Actually Helps** — arXiv `2608.06940` (Yang Shu). Nine judges
  carry roughly two judges' worth of independent information, and the entire
  accuracy gain concentrates on one-vote-margin queries (+10.4 to +23.3 pp).
  *A measured example of an aggregate metric being valid in the mean and blind
  where the effect lives — exactly the failure mode P7's evaluation-channel audit
  suspects.*
- **Agents Can Build Benchmarks. Approvals Missed What Probing Found: 22 Cases** —
  Research Square `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11407058/v1` (Pooria Arab). 22 agent-built
  benchmarks; no test failed on the last commit of 123 merged PRs and an LLM
  council approved 94, yet probing found defects in all 22; at least 13 recorded
  a strategy that ignored the task and still earned credit. *The most direct
  warning available about trusting our own green test suite.*
- **Sell Me This Stock: Unsafe Recommendation Drift in LLM Agents** — arXiv
  `2603.12564` (Wu; Koshiyama; Bulathwela et al.). Names the failure
  "evaluation blindness": NDCG scores risky and safe recommendations alike while
  65–99% of turns are risk-mismatched; 80% of citations across 1,840 turns copy
  manipulated tool output verbatim. *Same shape as P12, in a different domain.*
- **Benchmarking Reference-Free LLM Agent Robustness Under Schema, Policy, and
  Toolset Drift** — IEEE Access, DOI `10.1109/access.2026.3696096`.

## Gap G6 — goal drift: what to borrow (P6 is the weakest measured package)

H12 is inconclusive (held-out AUROC 0.8295 but early-warning rate 0.0). Useful
neighbours:

- **Drift No More? Context Equilibria in Multi-Turn LLM Interactions** — arXiv
  `2510.07777`; also OpenReview `LEUv8t5YMw`. Formalizes drift as turn-wise KL
  divergence against a goal-consistent reference model and fits a bounded
  stochastic process with restoring force. *A mechanism for the 0.0 early-warning
  rate: a monitor with no reference trajectory has nothing to measure against.*
- **Asymmetric Goal Drift in Coding Agents Under Value Conflict** — OpenReview,
  LLA 2026. Agents violate the system prompt **asymmetrically** depending on
  which competing value pressure applies.
- **Drift-Bench: Diagnosing Cooperative Breakdowns in LLM Agents under Input
  Faults** — arXiv `2602.02455` (Bao; Zhang; Jing et al.).
- **Stay Focused: Problem Drift in Multi-Agent Debate** — EACL Findings 2026.
  Generative tasks drift 76–89% of the time, high-complexity tasks 7–21%.
- **Behavioral Drift in Multi-Agent LLM Systems** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.6420858`
  (Jason Gagne). 200–500 turns; reports agent collapse, cascade propagation, and
  compensatory expansion.
- **Semantic Drift in Autonomous Agents** — DOI `10.53365/nrfhh.3575`. Runtime
  intent continuity middleware.
- **BioBlue** — runaway-optimizer-style failure modes in long-horizon control
  environments where models "lose context in structured ways".
- **Recursive AI Drift** — Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.20142976` (Reyes). Names the
  "coherence mirage": fluent surface preserved while the semantic anchor decays.
  *Candidate explanation for H12's 0.8295 AUROC with 0.0 early warning.*
- **Elaboration Drift** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.6480298` (Juhyeong Lee). Human-facing
  epistemic erosion; distinct from sycophancy.
- **DriftBench (coding-agent cheating)** — DOI `10.1109/caibda70336.2026.11621849`
  (Fang Hao). Specification-level constraint drift mutation.
- **ReflectiChain** — IEEE SPL, DOI `10.1109/lsp.2026.3726840` (Jia Luo).
  Grounding long-horizon planning against semantic-execution drift.
- **Get My Drift? Catching LLM Task Drift with Activation Deltas** — SaTML 2025,
  DOI `10.1109/satml64287.2025.00011`. White-box detection; *contrast case for
  our black-box monitor.*
- **Structural Drift as a System-Level Safety Failure** — medRxiv
  `10.64898/2026.03.19.26346371`. Message-level monitoring versus
  interaction-level risk.

## Gap G7 — world models (P4): only one usable neighbour

P4 is weak (ID accuracy 1.0, OOD 0.25, planning at parity with argmax). The
world-model query returned mostly biology. Usable:

- **A survey of world models for physical AI with uncertainty representation and
  control** — Discover AI, DOI `10.1007/s44163-026-02122-1`. Six design
  dimensions; discusses compounding error and planner exploitation of model
  error. *The compounding-error framing is what P4's OOD 0.25 is missing.*
- **Agent Planning with World Knowledge Model** — NeurIPS 2024 (Qiao; Fang;
  Zhang et al.). Prior + dynamic state knowledge.
- **Agentic-JEPA** — Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.20237490` (Rodrigo-Ginés). Reward-free
  JEPA world model for text environments with k-step lookahead.

## Gap G8 — harness/context integrity (relevant to our own tooling)

- **Context Compaction Provenance (CCP) Lab** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.6933161`
  (Michel Hjazeen). Treats compaction as a trust-boundary rewrite: attacker text
  retained anywhere versus promoted into trusted state.
- **MNEMA: A Memory-Native Episodic-Semantic Architecture for Persistent LLM
  Agents** — Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.20010220` (Kirste). Critiques the
  "file-based identity pattern" where state lives in side files the model is
  re-prompted with but seldom writes to. *Uncomfortably applicable: this
  repository persists as markdown.*
- **Tessera: Lossless-First Session Compression** — Research Square
  `10.21203/rs.3.rs-11000146/v1`. Lossless-first compression with a cold tier of
  exact originals behind content-addressed ids.
- **TWIST** — SSRN `10.2139/ssrn.7500098`. Benchmark for intervention quality in
  conversational memory.

## What this pass changes

Three things, recorded in `GROWTH-PLAN-20261101.md`:

1. The portfolio needs a **series** axis (P9), because "long series of tasks" is
   currently measured as 300 independent draws.
2. The portfolio needs a **metric-validity** axis (P12), because 190 passing
   tests constrain the harness, not the validity of the scores it emits.
3. The memory (P3) and self-evolution (P7) results need lifecycle and erosion
   extensions (P10, P11) before they can be described as claims about persistence.
